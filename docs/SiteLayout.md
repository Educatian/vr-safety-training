# Site Layout — "진짜 공사현장 다니는 느낌"

Goal: every day of the week should feel like **walking a real U.S. civil jobsite**, not visiting a training diorama. The map is laid out like a real **site logistics plan** (the drawing a GC posts in the trailer). Every experience happens inside this one continuous site. There are no portals, no floating signs and no training pads.

Project fiction: *Municipal Pump Station & Stormwater Tie-in*, a 2-storey pump-station building plus a new storm line that ties into the street. Site parcel **90 m × 70 m**. The street runs along the south side; the overhead distribution line runs along the north property line.

Plan drawing: `docs/images/site_plan.png`, generated by `Tools/layout/site_plan.py`. Coordinates below are in metres, with the origin at the SW corner, +x east and +y north. Unity uses x → x and y → z.

## 2. Walking feel (what makes it feel like you're *there*)

- **Real distances.** The trailer is about 35 m from the building and about 55 m from the trench. At a walk speed of 1.4 m/s (starting value), the site is big enough to *walk*, not teleport. Desktop sprint is capped at 2.5 m/s. VR uses smooth locomotion plus snap-turn, with teleport as a comfort option only.
- **Ground truth.** Each surface has its own footstep set and physics material: asphalt, crushed stone, compacted dirt, **mud** (slower, squelch), plywood deck, metal deck, scaffold planks and trench bedding stone. Terrain has micro-undulation, ruts and puddles that reflect the sky.
- **You are wearing PPE.** Hard-hat brim shadow and a very faint safety-glasses edge vignette on desktop (none in VR). Radio chatter comes over the radio, not the HUD.
- **The site is busy and indifferent to you.** Trucks run the haul loop on a schedule, the excavator digs, the crew works (carrying, cutting, tying rebar), a delivery arrives mid-shift, and a backup alarm is heard *before* the truck is seen.
- **Sound carries information.** Layers include the generator hum near temp power, a saw screaming at the cut station, excavator hydraulics, wind on the roof deck, street traffic beyond the fence and a distant siren. Everything is spatialized with distance falloff.
- **Atmosphere.** Morning light on Monday; hot midday with heat shimmer and a dust layer on Thursday; overcast on Friday. There are dust puffs behind trucks, dust from saw cutting (the silica hazard itself) and puddle splashes.
- **Discovery by occlusion.** Stockpiles, conex boxes, trailers and the building itself block sightlines. Hazards sit *inside* real work areas and are found by walking the site, as a real competent-person walk would find them.
- **No training furniture.** No floating labels, glowing pads, or colored hazard rings. The only UI is the tablet and the radio (GDD invariant).

## 3. Daily site evolution (the same map, a different job each day)

| Day | Building | Trench / civil | Yard and traffic | Active hazard areas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Staked footprint, excavation started | Locate marks fresh, trench not started | First deliveries; temp power set-up | Gate/PPE, temp power, trailer |
| Tue | Footings formed | **Trench open**, box in, pipe being laid | Spoil trucks loop; excavator swing | Trench, excavator, locate marks |
| Wed | Slab poured, **walls/frame + deck**, scaffold up | Trench partly backfilled | Lumber and steel deliveries | Deck edges, openings, ladders, scaffold, cords |
| Thu | **Roof** deck, skylight openings | Backfilled; surface restoration | **Crane pick** of the roof units; heat | Roof edge, skylights, suspended load, swing radius |
| Fri | Roof complete; **exterior slab pour** | Street tie-in open near the utilities | **Concrete pump truck near the overhead line** | Electrical, pump boom, GFCI/cords (capstone) |

Implementation: a `SitePhaseController` switches phase roots (`Phase_Mon` … `Phase_Fri`). Hazard pools are sampled on top of each phase (GDD §3/§5).

## 4. Realism checklist per zone (prop authenticity: PropBible)

- Every prop exists on real U.S. jobsites and follows PropBible dimensions, colors and markings.
- Every work area has **evidence of work**: offcuts, sawdust, tool bags, a water jug, cords and hose, a lunch cooler, stakes and string lines.
- Every hazard has a **plausible production reason**. For example, the guardrail midrail was removed to land a material pallet; the ladder was moved to reach a new area.
- Compliant look-alikes get the same wear level as the hazard version (the parity rule).
- Signage follows OSHA 1926.200 formats and is generic (no real company logos).
